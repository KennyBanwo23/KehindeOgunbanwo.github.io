Research · Final-Year Thesis, 2023

A Machine Learning Model for Heart Disease and Diabetes Prediction

Supervised by Prof. H.O.D. Longe, Augustine University

Compared K-Nearest Neighbours, Random Forest, Support Vector Machine and Naïve Bayes on two public clinical datasets: 1,025 heart disease records and the Pima Indians Diabetes dataset (768 patients). Models were evaluated with accuracy, recall and F1-score. KNN performed best for heart disease (87% accuracy) and Random Forest for diabetes (82.8%).

What I learnt: a high accuracy on small public data does not make a model ready for clinical use. Missed cases matter more than false alarms, and clinicians need to know how far to trust each individual prediction. This now drives my interest in reliable, privacy-preserving clinical machine learning.
